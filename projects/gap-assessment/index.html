NIST CSF 2.0 Gap Assessment

A scoped NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 assessment of 36 outcomes, six from each function. Each outcome has an observed current state, a maturity score against a target profile, a prioritized recommendation and an owner.

NIST CSF 2.0NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5ISO/IEC 27001:2022
OrganizationNovaCare Health Technologies (fictional)
PeriodMar – May 2026; status as of Oct 31, 2026
Prepared byEmmanuel Balogun

Engagement summary

Objective

Measure NovaCare's program against a CSF 2.0 target profile and produce a prioritized roadmap.

Scope

36 of the 106 CSF 2.0 subcategories, chosen for relevance to PHI protection and SOC 2 commitments.

Approach

Document review, 12 stakeholder interviews and inspection of system configurations and reports.

Criteria

Maturity 0–4 (Not performed, Initial, Repeatable, Defined, Managed). Target is 3 unless the outcome warrants otherwise.

36outcomes assessed, 6 per function
6fully implemented
1.6 / 3.0average current versus target maturity
14High-priority gaps on the roadmap

Conclusion and key findings

Overall rating
Needs improvement

Govern, Identify and Protect have working foundations. Detect and Recover are the weakest functions and drive most High-priority gaps.

DE.CM-03, DE.CM-06

Activity by insiders and vendors is not monitored

Record access in the support console and vendor VPN sessions are logged nowhere a person reviews. Neither would surface misuse of PHI.

DE.AE-03

The SIEM sees 62% of the log sources it needs

Cloud data events and identity risk signals are missing, so detections rely almost entirely on endpoint alerts.

RC.RP-01 to RC.RP-04

Recovery has never been exercised end to end

There is no agreed restore order, no backup integrity check and no criteria for declaring recovery complete.

PR.AA-03, PR.AA-05

Strong basics with privileged gaps

MFA covers all users and Entra access is reviewed quarterly, but privileged MFA is phishable and AWS roles are never reviewed.

Method and criteria

How the assessment was performed

  1. Select outcomes with stakeholders and set the target maturity for each.
  2. Interview control owners and request evidence for each outcome.
  3. Inspect configurations and reports to confirm what is actually in place.
  4. Rate status and current maturity; record the gap to target.
  5. Prioritize gaps by risk (linked to the enterprise register) and assign owners.
  6. Publish a roadmap and re-assess outcomes as remediation closes.

Maturity scale

LevelNameMeaning
0Not performedNo process or control in place
1InitialAd hoc; depends on individuals
2RepeatableDone consistently but not documented or measured
3DefinedDocumented, owned and followed across the organization
4ManagedMeasured, reported and improved
